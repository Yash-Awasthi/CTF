# CASE 71-C — Story Bible & Challenge Design

Internal truth document. Participants never see this in full — they reconstruct fragments of it.

---

## 1. Complete True History (1871–Present)

**Cycle 1 — 1871–1889**
Josiah Marrow, dollmaker in a small industrial town, begins producing unusually lifelike dolls on commission. Locals note he sometimes finishes a doll before meeting the child it "resembles." 1878: Alba Rourke, age 9, disappears. A doll matching her exactly — including a scar she received *after* the doll was sold — surfaces in Marrow's shop window. Thaddeus Grieve, a local constable turned private investigator, pursues the case obsessively for 11 years. 1889: Grieve vanishes mid-investigation. His casebook is never recovered intact. Marrow dies the same year, cause recorded as "natural."

**The Silent Decade — 1930s** (see §3)

**Cycle 2 — 1948–1954**
Marrow's collection resurfaces through a grandnephew, name lost to record damage. Peter Ilves, age 11, goes missing in 1952. A PI (name redacted — page torn from every surviving file) investigates. Notes end mid-sentence in 1954, pen stroke trailing off the page. No body, no arrest, no closure.

**Cycle 3 — 1968–1979**
Collection passes through at least two private sales. 1978: an unnamed woman disappears; her likeness is later identified as "Doll #6." Investigator identity lost — paper trail thinnest of all cycles, deliberately, it seems.

**Cycle 4 — ~1990s–2000s**
Silas Vale acquires the collection piecemeal over roughly a decade, believing himself a preserver of forgotten craft, not a participant in anything. No confirmed disappearance directly tied to this cycle — or one hasn't been found. Vale dies of natural causes, early 2010s. Estate scheduled for auction.

**Cycle 5 — 2015**
Mira Castellan, contracted to catalogue the Vale estate for auction, notices the doll anomalies. Investigates independently. Daniel Reyes (identity, not person — see §7) appears as colleague/helper. Evidence begins mutating around her. She identifies "Daniel" as her killer, is wrong in the way the participant will also be wrong, then disappears in 2015. Blackwood closes the file as unsolved 71-C.

**Cycle 6 — Now**
Participant assigned 71-C, eleven years cold. Cycle repeats.

## 2. Cycle Mechanics

- **Collector becomes involved:** always inherits or acquires the objects without seeking them out — never buys with intent, always "falls into it" (inheritance, estate sale, professional obligation).
- **Subject is chosen:** never chosen by the Collector. The doll/object anomaly precedes the disappearance — object exists first, disappearance follows within 1–4 years. Mechanism unknown even internally.
- **Investigator notices:** always someone with legitimate access to records (police, PI, archivist, in Mira's case an estate cataloguer) who crosses the anomaly by professional accident, not suspicion.
- **Witness's function:** a peripheral figure who sees one piece of the pattern, never the whole thing, and survives — becomes the reason fragments persist for the next cycle (a diary kept, a rumor repeated, a file misfiled instead of destroyed).
- **Successor determined:** whoever next gains legitimate access to the surviving fragments, unknowingly. Never recruited, never chosen by a person — the paper trail itself selects them.
- **What ends a cycle:** the Investigator's disappearance. Cycle formally closes when their case is marked unsolved and archived. The archiving act is what seeds the next cycle's fragments.

## 3. The Silent Decade (1930s)

**What's known:** a regional cluster of disappearances, never linked, never explained, no Marrow-collection object confirmed connected. Local rumor (in Cycle 2's investigator's notes, secondhand) says "the workshop things" changed hands during this period.

**What survives:** two newspaper clippings mentioning unrelated missing-persons cases with no collection connection ever established; a property tax record showing the Marrow estate briefly untaxed/ownerless.

**What went wrong (implied, never confirmed):** the chain may have broken — no clear Investigator role filled, no clear Successor. If true, this is the one time in recorded history THE CONTINUITY's doctrine failed.

**Permanently unexplained:** whether the disappearances were connected to the pattern at all, whether the "break" caused something worse, why records from this specific decade are unusually incomplete compared to every other cycle. This is not resolved anywhere in the CTF. It is planted as an anomaly in the Ledger (Q28) that the participant notices and cannot close.

## 4. Silas Vale — Complete History

Born 1940s (exact year deliberately vague in-game), only child of a shopkeeper family. Quiet, solitary, drawn to objects with history rather than people. Worked as an appraiser/auction-house cataloguer for decades — legitimate, unremarkable career. Began acquiring pieces of the old Marrow-lineage collection in the 1990s through estate sales, believing he was simply a serious collector of "folk-craft dolls and ephemera." Documented everything meticulously (this is why the collection survives intact enough for Mira to investigate).

**What he believed:** he was preserving forgotten craftsmanship and local history.
**What he discovered but never processed:** several catalog dates didn't add up — he noted the discrepancies as "cataloguing errors," filed them, moved on. He was intellectually adjacent to the truth and never crossed into it.
**What he misunderstood:** he thought the "impossible" dates were his own transcription mistakes, repeated across decades, rather than external interference.
**Why he matters:** he's the reason the physical evidence exists in one place for Mira to find — an unwitting archivist, not a participant in the deeper pattern. Dies of natural causes, no anomaly attached to his death.

## 5. Mira Castellan — Complete History

Freelance private investigator, contracted (not Blackwood staff) to catalogue and appraise the Vale estate for auction — routine work. Methodical, skeptical, not prone to superstition, which matters for how her notebook reads later (a skeptic's escalating unease is more convincing than a believer's).

**First anomaly:** a doll's provenance date predates its supposed maker's birth — she assumes clerical error.
**Investigation path:** pulls the thread anyway (professional thoroughness), finds the pattern across multiple owners, starts keeping a private notebook outside her official appraisal paperwork.
**Relationship with Daniel:** meets "Daniel Reyes," who identifies himself as a researcher with prior knowledge of the collection's provenance. He's helpful, credible, has access she doesn't. She trusts him for months.
**Changing evidence:** she's the first to notice — and document — that artifacts she's already logged change on reencounter. Writes about it obsessively, second-guessing her own memory in her final entries.
**Final discovery:** connects the pattern across historical owners, begins to suspect Daniel isn't what he claims, writes "Stop looking for people. Follow the names" as her last coherent note.
**Disappearance:** 2015, mid-investigation. Case goes cold. Her official file is later quietly reframed by Blackwood as a standard missing-person case — the reframing itself is a discoverable inconsistency (why does an appraisal contractor's disappearance have a full investigative case file at all?).

## 6. Daniel Reyes — The False Story (what the participant is meant to believe, Q9–24)

Presents as a documents researcher who assisted Vale's estate cataloguing and stayed on to help resolve outstanding provenance questions after Vale's death. Personable, a little guarded, plausible reasons for everything. Helps the participant for roughly the first two-thirds of the event — provides files, explains Mira's state of mind ("she became obsessed"), seems genuinely worried about her.

**Lies told:** understates how long he "knew" Mira; claims he met her only professionally when evidence later shows deeper access to her private notes; downplays his access to the archive.
**Suspicious actions surfacing gradually:** his access timestamps don't match his story; his "helpful" memo has an earlier, contradictory draft; a recovered recording has Mira naming him specifically as someone she feared.
**Apparent motive:** built from financial/access records — implication he manipulated Mira to protect what he knew about the collection's value or origin, then eliminated her when she got too close.
**Q24 conviction:** motive (protecting a secret), means (privileged access), opportunity (timeline places him at her last known location), and Mira's own recorded fear — the participant closes the case believing they've solved it. It should feel completely earned.

## 7. Daniel Reyes — The Truth (revealed Q25–27)

"Daniel Reyes" is not a person — it's a role/identity slotted into every cycle since at least Cycle 2 (confirmed instance; possibly older). Whoever occupies it in a given decade has a different face, but shares:

- The same name, presented with total conviction.
- The same handwriting characteristics (trained, not innate — implies deliberate instruction, by whom is unknown).
- The same core phrases, delivered as if original, actually inherited script — the "Daniel" of any cycle doesn't know he's reciting doctrine fragments.
- The same functional position: trusted colleague/helper to that cycle's Investigator, right up until suspicion turns.

**Do they know their role?** No — or not fully. Each "Daniel" believes his own cover story. He may sense fragments don't fully make sense (the phrases feel scripted even to him) but has no access to the historical pattern. He may himself be a Successor from an earlier cycle who was folded into the role rather than the Investigator track — this is never confirmed, deliberately.
**Why the recurrence:** implies an external mechanism installs the "Daniel" role each cycle, using whoever is nearby and suitably positioned — same ambiguity as the doll anomaly itself. No character in the story, including THE CONTINUITY's own members, can explain the mechanism.

## 8. Blackwood Investigative Bureau

**Founded:** publicly, decades ago, as an ordinary regional private-investigation and archival-services firm — legitimate cold-case work, corporate background checks, estate investigations (which is how it legitimately intersects with Vale's estate and, later, Mira's file).

**What it does:** real casework, real staff, real clients — this is not a front in the conspiracy-thriller sense.

**Do employees know about THE CONTINUITY?** No. Blackwood as an institution is unknowingly a recurring access layer — case files that touch the pattern tend to end up routed through Blackwood over the decades, not because Blackwood seeks them, but because the fragments (Witness-preserved diaries, misfiled reports, estate paperwork) keep surfacing through exactly the kind of channels Blackwood operates in.

**Mira's connection:** contracted, not staff — her file being a full Blackwood case at all is itself an inconsistency the participant can notice (why does a contractor's disappearance get this level of institutional file?).

**Why the participant receives the case:** cold-case reassignment, routine — the same mundane administrative mechanism that has, unknowingly, handed the file to a new Investigator every cycle.

## 9. THE CONTINUITY

**Not creators, not recruiters. Preservers of a chain neither they nor anyone else understands.**

**First appearance of the name:** earliest confirmed written use is a marginal note in the Cycle 2 file (1950s) — "the continuity must not break," unsigned. Whether the name predates this is unknown (possible link to the Silent Decade).

**Who currently operates it:** deliberately unspecified — the participant never meets a "member." Implied to be a small, informal, possibly self-selected group of former Witnesses/Successors who inherited awareness of the pattern without inheriting any explanation of it.

**What members actually know:** the role table (Collector/Subject/Investigator/Witness/Successor), that disruption to the chain historically correlates with worse outcomes (the Silent Decade), and nothing about mechanism or origin.

**What they believe:** that maintaining the chain — letting each cycle run its course, letting each Investigator either complete or vanish, preserving the fragments for the next Successor — is safer than interrupting it, based only on the one time (apparently) it stopped.

**Why they obey the doctrine:** fear of the alternative, not understanding of the phenomenon. "THE INVESTIGATION MUST CONTINUE" is a rule with no explanation attached, precisely because no one alive has one.

## 13. Daniel's False Conviction — Complete Evidence Chain (for Q21–24)

- **Motive:** financial/provenance record (Q21 adjacent) implies Daniel had reason to suppress the collection's true origin — value or liability tied to Lot 47's impossible dating.
- **Means:** elevated, undisclosed archive access (Q21) — he could reach and alter records Mira couldn't.
- **Opportunity:** transit/access logs place him near Mira's last known location during the impossible alibi window (Q22).
- **Behavioral evidence:** contradicting memo draft (Q15), inconsistent account of how long he knew Mira.
- **Direct evidence:** Mira's recovered recording names him specifically as someone she feared (Q23).
- **Synthesis (Q24):** all five threads converge without contradiction *at this point in the investigation* — deliberately, so the solve feels complete and correct.

## 14. Q25–27 Design — Destroying Q24, Backward from Mira's Line

Target line: **"Stop looking for people. Follow the names."**

- **Q25 must destroy "means/identity":** if Daniel Reyes appears in 1963, the *specific person* convicted in Q24 cannot be the same individual who committed the 2015 disappearance — identity itself breaks.
- **Q26 must destroy "singularity":** if there are multiple Daniel Reyeses across decades with shared handwriting/phrases, "Daniel Reyes" was never a suspect — it's a role. Motive and means attached to a person collapse entirely.
- **Q27 must destroy "method":** Mira already saw through this — her own conclusion, recovered last, tells the participant the correct next move (stop chasing individuals, trace the recurring name/role instead) and sets up Q28.

Each of the three is a small, specific, checkable fact — not a monologue — matching the "Sherlock-style inconsistency" requirement.

## 16. Q28 Design

**Discovery path:** following the "follow the names" instruction from Q27, the participant locates a historical records database (not literally called "the Ledger" until reconstructed).

**Damaged/missing:** several rows are fragmented — corrupted fields, redacted names, one entire cycle (Silent Decade) mostly empty.

**Reconstruction task:** correlate names/roles recovered across Q1–27 (Marrow, Grieve, Ilves, Vale, Mira, Daniel instances) against database fragments to rebuild the role table.

**Realization reached:** five roles repeat across 150+ years; the participant's own case (Cycle 6) is already a row in the table, incomplete.

**Exact answer:** `SUCCESSOR` — the role the participant is being asked to identify as the missing/pending field that structurally matches their own position (validated via the reconstructed table, not a lookup).

**Player-facing prompt (forces role-table thinking, not archive browsing):** the terminal does not present a flat document dump. It presents an empty five-column template —

```
COLLECTOR | SUBJECT | INVESTIGATOR | WITNESS | SUCCESSOR
```

— one row per cycle, mostly blank. The participant fills cells using names/dates recovered from Q1–27 (Marrow, Grieve, Ilves, Vale, Mira, Daniel instances), and the interface only accepts a completed table before revealing the current-cycle row. The final prompt is explicitly:

```
FIVE ROLES REPEAT. IDENTIFY THE ROLE CURRENTLY UNASSIGNED IN THIS CASE.
```

This forces pattern-completion across the whole table rather than a single lookup — matching a corrupted-archive puzzle, not a reading-comprehension one.

## 18. Q29 — The Experience

**On screen:** a Blackwood evidence-correlation terminal, distinct visual style from the rest of the platform — the participant is explicitly told this draws on evidence gathered throughout the entire case.

**Artifacts available:** all ten secondary anomalies, presented as a correlation board (participant must have actually found them during play — no re-serving of content they skipped).

**Reasoning required:** assemble the doctrine, then infer the responsible system's name from its function (preserving the chain) rather than the doctrine text containing the name literally.

**Prompt:** `IDENTIFY THE SYSTEM RESPONSIBLE FOR MAINTAINING THE LEDGER.`

**Answer:** `THE CONTINUITY`

**Immediately after submission:** no congratulation message. Interface hard-cuts — Blackwood branding removed, replaced by a stripped-down "THE CONTINUITY" interface shell for Q30.

## 19. Q30 Design

**Codename placement:** assigned silently at Q1, referenced nowhere explicitly again — it should appear only in small, easy-to-miss UI chrome (e.g., a session label) so a careful participant notices it persisted the whole time without being told to remember it.

**Final prompt screen:**
```
CONTINUITY RECORD: CURRENT
COLLECTOR: COMPLETE
SUBJECT: COMPLETE
INVESTIGATOR: COMPLETE
WITNESS: COMPLETE
SUCCESSOR: PENDING

FINAL QUERY
IDENTIFY THE SUBJECT.
```

**Accepted answer rules:** exact match (case-insensitive, trimmed) to the participant's own codename only. Common wrong guesses (`MIRA CASTELLAN`, `DANIEL REYES`, `SILAS VALE`) should return a distinct "incorrect" state, not a generic error — reinforces the misdirection without giving anything away.

**Ending text on success:**
```
SUBJECT IDENTIFIED.
LEDGER UPDATED.
CASE 71-C CLOSED.
CONTINUITY STATUS: ACTIVE.

WELCOME, [CODENAME].
```
