# CASE 71-C — Worldbuilding and Challenge Design Guide

This document is the creative-development companion to the CASE 71-C Story Bible.

The Story Bible defines the internal truth of the world.

This guide defines what must be designed for each challenge before implementation begins.

The purpose is to prevent Phase 13 from becoming a collection of disconnected CTF puzzles. Every challenge must function simultaneously as:

1. A technical challenge.
2. A narrative event.
3. A piece of evidence.
4. A consequence of the previous discovery.
5. A reason to continue investigating.
6. Where applicable, a contribution toward the Q29 climax.

---

# 1. Core Design Principle

The participant must never feel that they are completing thirty unrelated CTF challenges.

They are investigating CASE 71-C.

The participant should never encounter:

> Challenge 8: SQL Injection

Instead, they encounter:

> The auction catalogue refuses to display Lot 47.

The technical vulnerability exists underneath the story.

Similarly:

* HTML source becomes an improperly published case file.
* IDOR becomes an old directory with enumerable records.
* SQL injection becomes a vulnerable archive search system.
* EXIF inspection becomes photographic evidence analysis.
* Client-side manipulation becomes bypassing a flawed provenance system.
* Audio forensics becomes recovering Mira's damaged recording.
* Cryptography becomes decoding Mira's private notes.
* File comparison becomes proving that evidence has changed.
* Cross-challenge synthesis becomes reconstructing the historical pattern.

The participant receives evidence, documents, systems, photographs, recordings, archives, and situations.

They do not receive puzzles.

# 2. Narrative Structure

## Act 1 — Follow Mira

Questions 1–8.

The participant receives Mira Castellan's cold case and reconstructs the final investigation she conducted before disappearing.

The central question is:

> What was Mira investigating?

The participant follows:

Mira → notebook → address → estate → Marrow → directory → photograph → auction → Lot 47.

The challenges should be easy to easy-medium.

The participant should gain confidence while becoming interested in Mira's disappearance.

Nothing should yet suggest THE CONTINUITY or the full historical pattern.

---

## Act 2 — The Collection

Questions 9–15.

The participant investigates Vale's collection and discovers that its history is impossible.

The central question becomes:

> How old is this collection, and why are people connected to it disappearing?

Daniel Reyes enters as a helpful, credible character.

The participant follows:

Lot 47 → catalogue → Doll #6 → provenance → Daniel → vanished owner → impossible acquisition date → Daniel's explanation.

Daniel should initially reduce uncertainty.

He provides access.

He explains context.

He offers rational interpretations.

By the end of the act, however, the participant should notice that Daniel knows more than he admits.

---

## Act 3 — The Haunting

Questions 16–20.

The investigation becomes personal.

The central question becomes:

> Is the evidence changing around me?

Previously encountered artifacts return altered.

A photograph changes.

A supposedly abandoned website updates.

A voicemail references participant-specific information.

A previously accessed file changes.

Mira's notebook reveals that she experienced the same phenomenon.

This act should produce uncertainty rather than answers.

The participant should begin doubting:

* the evidence;
* Daniel;
* Blackwood;
* Mira's sanity;
* their own memory.

Nothing should yet reveal the historical role structure.

---

## Act 4 — Daniel Reyes

Questions 21–24.

The participant stops investigating the supernatural pattern and focuses on a human suspect.

The central question becomes:

> Did Daniel Reyes make Mira disappear?

The participant discovers:

* motive;
* privileged access;
* dishonesty;
* a broken alibi;
* direct accusation from Mira.

Everything converges.

Question 24 should feel like the real ending.

The participant identifies:

> DANIEL REYES

The system accepts the answer.

The case closes.

The participant should experience genuine satisfaction.

Only after that should the story continue.

---

## Act 5 — The Cracks

Questions 25–27.

Three discoveries destroy the solved case.

The central question becomes:

> Who, or what, is Daniel Reyes?

Question 25 destroys Daniel's identity.

Question 26 destroys Daniel's singularity.

Question 27 destroys the participant's investigative method.

Mira's final instruction becomes:

> STOP LOOKING FOR PEOPLE.

> FOLLOW THE NAMES.

This launches the participant toward the historical pattern.

---

## Act 6 — The Ledger

Question 28.

The participant follows recurring names across history.

The central question becomes:

> Has this happened before?

The answer is yes.

Repeatedly.

The participant reconstructs the five roles:

* Collector;
* Subject;
* Investigator;
* Witness;
* Successor.

The participant discovers that their own current investigation already exists as an incomplete Ledger row.

The answer is:

> SUCCESSOR

The participant now understands the structure.

They still do not know what maintains it.

---

## Act 7 — The Continuity

Question 29.

This is the climax.

The participant correlates anomalies accumulated throughout the investigation.

The central question becomes:

> What maintains the Ledger?

The participant reconstructs the doctrine:

> PEOPLE CHANGE.

> NAMES REMAIN.

> RECORDS CHANGE.

> ROLES REMAIN.

> THE INVESTIGATION MUST CONTINUE.

The participant identifies:

> THE CONTINUITY

Blackwood disappears.

The real interface appears.

---

## Act 8 — Current

Question 30.

No technical challenge.

No new artifact.

No historical reconstruction.

One incomplete record.

One question:

> IDENTIFY THE SUBJECT.

The answer is the participant's own codename.

# 4. Mandatory Design Template for Every Challenge

Before implementing any challenge, complete this template.

## Challenge Number and Title

`Q__ — TITLE`

## Opening Scene

What does the participant see immediately after entering the challenge?

## Artifact or System

What document, website, photograph, recording, database, archive, or interface exists?

## Previous-Question Connection

Exactly why did solving the previous challenge cause the participant to access this challenge?

## Participant's Objective

What does the participant believe they are trying to discover?

## Initial Belief

What does the participant currently believe about the case?

## Actual Discovery

What new fact does this challenge establish?

## Technical Technique

What CTF skill is required?

## Exact Solving Process

Write every step a participant must perform from opening the challenge to deriving the answer.

## Exact Answer

What answer is accepted?

## Personalization

Which elements differ between participants?

## Mutable State

Does this artifact change later?

If yes:

* where does it return;
* what changes;
* what remains identical;
* why might a careful participant notice?

## Q29 Contribution

Does this challenge contain a secondary anomaly?

If yes:

* what is it;
* why does it appear irrelevant;
* how does it contribute to Q29?

## Hint 1

A conceptual nudge.

## Hint 2

A stronger technical direction.

## Difficulty

Rate from 1–5.

## Expected Solve Time

Estimate realistically.

## Narrative Purpose

Why does this challenge exist in the story?

## Next-Question Connection

Exactly how does the answer or discovery cause the participant to investigate the next challenge?

# 5. Challenge Quality Checklist

Before accepting any challenge, verify all of the following.

## Narrative

* Does solving the previous challenge naturally lead here?
* Does this challenge reveal something new?
* Does solving it create a reason to continue?
* Is the artifact believable inside the CASE 71-C world?
* Does the participant act like an investigator rather than a CTF player?
* Is Mira, Daniel, the Collection, or the historical pattern meaningfully advanced?

## Technical

* Is the required technique appropriate for beginners?
* Is there a clear intended solution?
* Are accidental alternate solutions acceptable?
* Can the challenge be solved entirely in a browser where required?
* Is the answer deterministic?
* Is personalization compatible with the existing challenge engine?
* Does the challenge avoid requiring paid infrastructure?

## Fairness

* Is every necessary clue available?
* Does the challenge require reasoning rather than guessing?
* Is specialized knowledge explained through context where necessary?
* Are hints useful without revealing the answer?
* Does the participant know what kind of answer to submit?
* Can a participant recover after taking a wrong investigative direction?

## Pacing

* Is this technique too similar to the previous challenge?
* Have there been too many file-inspection challenges consecutively?
* Have there been too many encoding challenges?
* Is the difficulty curve appropriate?
* Does the challenge provide enough narrative reward for the effort required?

## Security and Anti-Cheat

* Can answers be personalized?
* Can copied answers be detected where attribution is enabled?
* Does the client receive only public challenge data?
* Is validation server-side?
* Are private answers absent from browser bundles?
* Does the challenge avoid leaking later content?

# 6. Recommended Drafting Order

Do not design all thirty challenges simultaneously.

## Batch 1 — Q1–8

Objective:

Make the participant care about Mira and discover the impossible collection timeline.

Focus on:

* Blackwood;
* Mira;
* Marrow;
* the estate;
* the photograph;
* Lot 47.

## Batch 2 — Q9–15

Objective:

Make the Collection disturbing and Daniel trustworthy.

Focus on:

* Doll #6;
* provenance;
* previous owners;
* Daniel's personality;
* Daniel's rational explanation.

## Batch 3 — Q16–20

Objective:

Make the investigation feel personal and unstable.

Focus on:

* changed artifacts;
* personalized evidence;
* memory uncertainty;
* Mira experiencing the same phenomenon.

## Batch 4 — Q21–24

Objective:

Construct an airtight false conviction.

Focus on:

* motive;
* means;
* opportunity;
* broken alibi;
* direct accusation.

## Batch 5 — Q25–27

Objective:

Destroy the false conviction with three precise facts.

Focus on:

* identity;
* recurrence;
* investigative method.

## Batch 6 — Q28

Objective:

Design the Ledger as a major standalone experience.

Do not treat Q28 as an ordinary challenge.

## Batch 7 — Q29

Objective:

Design the climax backward.

Q29 should be completely solved on paper before implementing any of its contributing fragments in earlier challenges.

## Batch 8 — Q30

Objective:

Remove complexity.

The ending should depend on recognition, not technical difficulty.

# 7. Final Design Rule

Every challenge must answer three questions:

1. Why is the participant here?
2. What do they learn?
3. Why must they continue?

If any challenge cannot answer all three clearly, it is not ready for implementation.

The complete investigation should form one causal chain:

CASE 71-C

→ Mira

→ Vale estate

→ Marrow

→ the Collection

→ Doll #6

→ Daniel Reyes

→ changing evidence

→ Mira experienced it too

→ Daniel appears guilty

→ CASE CLOSED

→ Daniel existed before Daniel

→ recurring identities

→ FOLLOW THE NAMES

→ the Ledger

→ repeating roles

→ THE CONTINUITY

→ CURRENT

→ the participant.
